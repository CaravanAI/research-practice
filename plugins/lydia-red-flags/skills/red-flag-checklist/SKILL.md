# Red-flag checklist

Read the annual report (the `.txt` copy in the company's `sources/` folder) and check each item below. For every flag you find, record what the report says and where.

## The items

1. **Auditor.** A change of auditor, or an opinion that is qualified (the auditor says the accounts have a problem).
2. **Going concern.** Language that the company may not be able to keep operating.
3. **Dividends above earnings.** Dividends paid larger than the profit for the year.
4. **Related-party deals.** Deals with insiders or their companies.
5. **Receivables.** Money owed by customers growing faster than revenue.
6. **Debt coming due.** Borrowings that must be repaid soon.
7. **Board and key managers.** Changes in the board or in key managers.
8. **Pledged shares.** Big shareholders using their shares as security for loans. If the share price falls, a lender can force a sale.
9. **One-off gains.** Big one-off gains that boost profit, so profit looks better than the normal business earns.

Items 8 and 9 are the analyst's own additions.

## How to check

- For each item, search the full text of the `.txt` copies for the key terms before you decide. Reading only the contents page is not enough.
- If an item has no flag, still list it with "No flag found" and the terms you searched. Do not leave it out.
- Check the unit on every table (for example RM thousand or RM million) and give the currency with every figure.

## Label every flag

Each flag carries exactly one label:

- **Sourced**: the report says it. Give the file and page.
- **Inferred**: you worked it out from figures in the report. Show the inputs and the formula.
- **Not in the sources**: the report does not say. Say which document would answer it.

Cite the original PDF file name and the page number from the `=== PDF PAGE n ===` markers, for example `bphb-iar2025.pdf, p. 30`. The `.txt` copy is the same source as the PDF.

## Severity

- **High**: could threaten the company's ability to operate or to pay its dividend.
- **Medium**: needs a question to management before anyone relies on the numbers.
- **Low**: worth a note, no action yet.

## What comes back

A table grouped by severity (High, then Medium, then Low). Columns: item number and name, what the report says (with the figure, currency and unit), file and page, label. Then a short list of items with no flag found, with the terms searched.

No buy, sell or hold, no price targets, no view on whether the shares are cheap or expensive. Report what the document says and where.
